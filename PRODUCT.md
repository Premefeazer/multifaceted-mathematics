# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students aged about 12 to 18 (middle and high school) who study math on their own, often in short gaps between classes. They decide within seconds whether a page is worth their time, and they notice when something talks down to them. Teachers are a secondary audience: they look for something trustworthy they can recommend or assign.

## Product Purpose

Multifaceted Mathematics is an interactive math-learning platform that covers eight topics in order, from logic and set theory to calculus basics. A lesson is a short sequence of screens: you make a guess first, then see why. Success is a student who understands an idea by manipulating it, not by reading about it. Today the site is a landing page and a topic map, and every lesson is marked as coming soon. Accounts, streaks and saved progress come later.

## Brand Personality

Sharp mentor, quietly elegant, with a little play. It talks to teenagers directly and respectfully, in short, precise sentences. The look is calm and confident, built around one geometric object (the 20-faced icosahedron), with moments of delight that come from the math itself and not from decoration.

## Anti-references

- The AI template look: gradient heroes, glow blobs, rows of identical icon cards, filler taglines under every heading.
- The dry textbook: walls of text, worksheet grey, nothing to touch.
- Corporate SaaS: buzzwords, stock illustration, a demo-request tone.

Reference points: Brilliant (guess-first interactive lessons with a grown-up polish), 3Blue1Brown (math shown through geometry and motion, serious about the ideas), Desmos (direct manipulation, you drag and the math responds).

## Design Principles

1. Show the math, don't describe it. A topic, a lesson screen or a shape explains itself; no caption restates what the reader can see.
2. Let people touch it. Prefer direct manipulation (drag, tap, test a guess) over explanation.
3. Every element has a job. A pattern or an animation earns its place by teaching, orienting or confirming; decoration for its own sake is cut.
4. Restraint with one confident object. Calm surfaces, hairline borders, one hero shape, one highlight color.
5. Be honest about status. No invented numbers, reviews or logos; anything unfinished says Coming soon.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible focus rings, full keyboard use. Every animation (the hero shape, the ticker, the headline line) has a reduced-motion alternative. Fonts are chosen for legibility of math characters (Atkinson Hyperlegible for body text). Color never carries meaning alone, and tap targets are at least 44 px.
